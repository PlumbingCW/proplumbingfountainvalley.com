When a clogged drain is an emergency

A slow sink can wait for a daytime visit. Water coming up in a tub when the toilet flushes, sewage smell indoors or several fixtures backing up at once cannot, because those signs point to a blocked main line and the water has nowhere else to go.

While you wait for the plumber, stop using every drain in the house, including the washer and dishwasher. Move valuables off the floor near the affected fixture, keep children and pets out of the wet area and take photos for your insurance company. Our line at (714) 750-8637 is open 24/7.

How we clear a drain

Most residential clogs are cleared with a motorized drain cable, often called snaking. The cable carries a cutting head into the line, breaks through the blockage and pulls debris back out. For a bathroom sink or tub, the plumber usually works through the drain opening or a cleanout. For a main line, the cable goes in through an outdoor cleanout, which is the capped pipe near the foundation.

If the cable keeps hitting the same spot or the clog returns within weeks, a sewer camera shows what is really in the pipe. Roots, a bellied section, a cracked joint or years of grease all need different fixes, and clearing the water once does not solve any of them. Read more about the camera step on our sewer inspection page.

Signs your drain needs professional cleaning

These are the symptoms callers describe most often. Any two together usually mean the problem is past what a plunger can fix.

Gurgling after a flush

Air is being pushed past a partial blockage in the main line. It often shows up in a tub or shower drain nearest the main.

More than one fixture slow

One slow sink is usually local. A slow sink, tub and toilet together points to the shared line.

Water coming up elsewhere

Running the washer and seeing water in a floor drain or tub means the main line is restricted.

Odor that does not go away

A persistent sewer smell can come from a dry trap, but with slow drains it points to buildup or a leak in the drain line.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen sinks are the most common single-fixture call. Grease cools inside the pipe and coats the walls, food scraps catch on the coating, and over months the opening narrows until water pools. A garbage disposal can hide the problem because it grinds food small enough to travel a few feet before it settles.

A plumber clears the line with a cable and flushes it with hot water, then checks the p-trap and the dishwasher connection since both feed the same drain. If the disposal itself is jammed or leaking, see our garbage disposal repair page.

Why drains clog again in Fountain Valley homes

Fountain Valley's housing grew fast through the 1960s, according to the city's history page, so many homes have drain lines that are now more than 50 years old. Older lines are more likely to have rough joints, small offsets and root entry points, and each of those catches debris. A line that clears and then clogs again in a few weeks is telling you something.

Grease and wipes cause most repeat clogs, but not all of them. If the plumber's camera finds a belly where water pools, no amount of cabling fixes it, and the honest options are a repair or a regular cleaning schedule. We lay out those trade-offs on site before any work starts, and a bad section of pipe is covered on our sewer line repair page.

Cable, jetting or camera: choosing the right method

A cable is the right first step for most clogs because it is fast and it works on soft and moderate blockages. Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the pipe walls and suits grease buildup and some root growth. A camera does not clear anything, but it shows the cause and tells you whether you are dealing with a one-time clog or a failing pipe.

Ask the plumber which method is planned and why. The answer should come with a reason that fits what you described. Related lines such as whole sewer line cleaning are worth discussing when several fixtures are affected.

The rules behind drain work

Drain, waste and vent work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Clearing a clog does not need a permit, but changing pipe layout or replacing a section usually does, and the plumber will tell you which side of that line your job falls on.

Drain line leaks and shower or tub clogs

A drain that clears but leaves a damp cabinet, stained ceiling below a bathroom or musty smell may have a leak in the drain line itself. Water that only appears when the fixture drains is a different clue from a supply leak, and a plumber can test each separately.

Shower and tub clogs are mostly hair and soap. A hair snare or a short cable pass clears them, and the plumber checks the overflow connection while the fixture is open.
