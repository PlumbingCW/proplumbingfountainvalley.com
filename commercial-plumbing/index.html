When a commercial plumbing problem is an emergency

Sewage in a restroom, a kitchen drain that backs up during service, a burst line over merchandise or a water heater that fails in a food business all affect operations and health compliance. Close the shutoff that serves the area, stop using affected fixtures and protect stock and equipment.

Call (714) 750-8637 and tell the dispatcher the type of business, the fixture and whether customers or staff are present. The 24/7 line sends a plumber as soon as one is free, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

What commercial plumbing covers

Commercial buildings have longer drain runs, larger water heaters, more fixtures and different code rules than houses. A main line may serve several tenants. A restaurant adds grease handling and floor drains. A medical or dental office adds specific waste and water quality needs.

We handle drain cleaning, jetting, leak location, water heater work, fixture repair and installation, and sewer line camera inspection. For larger projects such as tenant improvements, scope is discussed and quoted before work begins.

Working around your hours

Many commercial jobs are best done outside business hours to avoid downtime. The plumber can schedule early, late or weekend work where it fits, and will coordinate with the manager or landlord for access.

For tenants, ask who is responsible under your lease before authorizing work. The owner may need to approve repairs to building systems.

Grease, backflow and compliance

Food businesses commonly have grease interceptors or traps that need regular cleaning. Cities and sanitation districts set the rules on grease control and cleaning records. Ask the City of Fountain Valley or the sanitation district what applies to your kitchen.

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination and are tested on a schedule set by the water provider. Your water provider can tell you the requirement, and a certified tester performs the test.

What not to do before the plumber arrives

Do not run a dishwasher or hot-line equipment on a backed-up drain, do not pour chemical cleaners into standing water and do not let staff mop around live outlets. Commercial work follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which also covers grease interceptors.

Landlords and property managers

If you manage several properties, tell the dispatcher so notes on access, shutoff locations and prior repairs can be kept together. Recurring problems in older buildings are worth a camera inspection of the main line.

A written note of findings after each visit helps with tenant communication and insurance.
