Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup carries bacteria into the house, so treat any standing sewage as a health hazard. Stop running water in sinks, tubs, washers and dishwashers, and do not flush. Keep children and pets out, and avoid walking through the water in bare feet.

Open windows if the smell is strong, and take photos for your insurance company. Our emergency line at (714) 750-8637 is open 24/7. Cleanup and sanitizing after a backup is a restoration job we do not provide.

What causes a sewer line to fail

The sewer lateral runs from the house to the city main. Roots enter at joints and cracks and thicken into a mat. Pipe can crack from ground movement or shift out of alignment so that water pools in a low spot called a belly. Old clay or cast iron can corrode or collapse.

Because Fountain Valley's housing boom came in the 1960s, many laterals are now over half a century old. That does not mean yours is failing, but it is a reason to run a camera when drains keep clogging.

Signs of a sewer line problem

These symptoms often appear before a full backup.

Multiple slow drains

Branch lines drain into one main, so trouble in the main shows up everywhere.

Sewage smell outdoors

A cracked line can leak into the soil and release odor near the cleanout.

Green or soggy patches

A leaking lateral can fertilize or saturate one stretch of lawn.

Repeated clogs

A clog that returns within weeks suggests roots or a damaged section.

Repair options

A spot repair replaces a short damaged section by digging down to it. Trenchless pipe lining inserts a resin-coated liner through the existing pipe and cures it into a new pipe-within-a-pipe, which can avoid digging up the yard. Full replacement is for pipes that have collapsed or are too damaged to line.

Each approach has trade-offs. Digging costs more in landscaping. Lining needs the pipe to be clear and relatively intact. Your plumber should show you the camera footage and explain why one option fits better.

Permits and standards for sewer work

Sewer repair follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and digging in the yard or the street usually needs a city permit and an inspection. The Fountain Valley Building Division at 714-593-4429 can tell you what applies to your address.

City main or your lateral?

The city or the Orange County Sanitation District is generally responsible for the public sewer main, and the property owner is generally responsible for the lateral that serves the home. If every house on the street is backing up, call the city. If only your home is affected, the lateral is more likely the cause.

Ask the City of Fountain Valley what applies to your address before you commit to a large repair. A camera video helps show where the problem sits.
