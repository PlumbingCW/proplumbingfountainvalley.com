What the city reports about your water

The City of Fountain Valley's 2026 Consumer Confidence Report covers 2025 data. It lists average hardness of 217 milligrams per liter, or 13 grains per gallon, with a range of 10 to 15 grains. The city's own FAQ describes water with 10 grains or more as hard. The report also notes that in 2025 the city supplied groundwater only.

Hardness varies by source and delivery area, so the number at your tap may differ. Test your tap, and ask the city for its latest annual report to see current figures.

Signs of hard water at home

Scale shows up in the same places in most homes.

White crust on fixtures

Calcium and magnesium dry on faucets, showerheads and glass.

Spotted dishes

Mineral spots appear after the dishwasher dries.

Short-lived heaters

Scale insulates elements and tank floors, causing noise and failure.

Dry skin or stiff laundry

Minerals interfere with soap and leave residue.

Testing and choosing a system

A hardness test kit gives a quick figure, and a lab test covers other items. Decide what you want to solve first, whether it is scale, taste or a specific contaminant. Your water provider's annual report lists what is in the supply, and that helps you avoid paying for treatment you do not need.

The plumber sizes the system to household size and flow. A softener that is too small regenerates too often and wastes salt.

Local rules and discharge

Some California communities restrict salt-based softeners because of the salt they send to wastewater. Ask the City of Fountain Valley or the sanitation district what applies at your address before you buy. The plumber can explain what a drain connection requires.

If rules are strict, a salt-free conditioner or a filtration-only plan may be the right path.

The rules on where softener discharge may go are set locally, so the City of Fountain Valley or the sanitation district is the place to ask. Plumbing connections follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Effects on the water heater

Softened water helps tank heaters last longer and keeps tankless units cleaner. If you already have scale in the tank, a flush before the softener goes in helps. See our water heater repair page for symptoms of scale.

A softener does not replace maintenance, but it lowers how fast scale builds.
