When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank that is leaking from its body cannot be repaired, and the leak usually gets worse. If water is pooling, you are looking at floor damage, mold risk and a flooded garage within hours.

Turn off the power or gas to the heater, close the cold-water shutoff above it and open a hot-water faucet somewhere in the house to relieve pressure. Then call (714) 750-8637. Our line is open 24/7, and we plan the replacement for the soonest appointment available.

How replacement works

The plumber drains the old tank, disconnects water, gas or power and vent connections, and removes the unit. The new heater is set, leveled and connected with new flexible connectors where needed. Gas units get a leak test at the fittings, and electric units get a power check.

The relief valve and its discharge pipe are checked or replaced, and an expansion tank is recommended where the plumbing calls for one. The last step is filling the tank, purging air and confirming hot water at the fixtures.

Signs it is time to replace instead of repair

A single symptom is not always a replacement trigger, but these usually point that way.

Leak from the tank body

The steel vessel has corroded through. A new valve or fitting cannot fix this.

Rusty hot water

Rust coming only from the hot side often means the tank is corroding inside.

Age over 10 to 12 years

Even working tanks near the end of typical life can fail without warning.

Repeated repairs

Two or three repairs in a year tend to cost more than a replacement does.

Hard water and your new heater

Fountain Valley's average hardness is 13 grains per gallon in the city's 2026 report, with a range of 10 to 15. That is hard water by the city's own 10 grain mark. A new tank filling with hard water starts building scale from the first heat cycle, so ask the plumber about the anode rod, a yearly flush and whether a water softener is worth pricing.

Tankless units are even more sensitive, because scale inside the heat exchanger cuts flow and triggers error codes. A descaling schedule is part of owning one here.

Permits and what to expect

The City of Fountain Valley's Building Division, at 10200 Slater Avenue, states that work on plumbing and gas systems goes through the building official's permit process. The Permit Center portal handles applications electronically. Permit rules vary, so ask the Building Division at 714-593-4429 what applies to your replacement.

The plumber can explain what the job needs, but the city makes the final call on its own rules. Counter hours at the Building Division are Monday through Thursday, 7 a.m. to 6 p.m., and closed Fridays, so plan questions around that.

What not to do with a failed heater

Do not relight a pilot on a tank that is leaking, do not cap or plug the relief valve and do not drain a hot tank onto carpet. The California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC, requires a relief valve and discharge line on every tank, and California law requires bracing against earthquake movement. A new install should meet both.

Seismic strapping and safety

Water heaters in California are commonly secured with straps to the wall studs to reduce the risk of tipping in an earthquake. Ask the plumber what applies to your install and have it done while the unit is open and accessible.

A heater that is not strapped, has a rusted base or sits on an uneven platform is something to correct at replacement time.
