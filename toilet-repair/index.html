When a toilet problem is an emergency

A toilet that is overflowing, leaking at the base or flooding a bathroom floor is an emergency. Reach behind the bowl, find the small oval valve on the wall or floor and turn it clockwise to stop the supply. Lift the tank lid and raise the float if the valve will not turn.

Do not flush again. If several fixtures back up with it, stop all water use and call (714) 750-8637. The line is open 24/7, and a plumber will clear the line and repair the toilet.

Why toilets run, leak and clog

Most toilet repairs come down to a handful of parts. A worn flapper lets water leak into the bowl and makes the fill valve cycle. A cracked fill valve can run constantly. A failed wax ring leaks at the floor, and a loose bolt lets the bowl rock.

A clog in the toilet itself is different from a clog in the drain. If a plunger clears it, the problem is local. If water backs up in a tub or floor drain at the same time, the clog is in the main line, covered on our sewer line cleaning page.

Symptoms and likely causes

Matching the symptom saves a trip and a guess.

Runs after flushing

The flapper is not sealing or the fill valve is not shutting off.

Water at the base

The wax ring or the supply connection has failed.

Weak or slow flush

Mineral buildup in the rim jets or a partial clog.

Rocks when you sit

Loose closet bolts or an uneven floor. Over time this breaks the wax seal.

Hard water and toilet parts

The City of Fountain Valley reports average water hardness of 13 grains per gallon for 2025. Hard water leaves mineral deposits in the rim jets and around the fill valve, which can weaken flushing and cause slow tank refills. Cleaning the jets and replacing the fill valve are common maintenance steps here.

A water softener reduces scale throughout the house, though it is a larger decision than a toilet repair.

New toilet installation

When it is time to replace, the plumber removes the old toilet, inspects the flange for damage, sets a new wax ring or seal and secures the bowl. Supply lines and the shutoff valve are replaced if they are old or corroded.

Ask about the rough-in measurement, which is the distance from the wall to the flange center, since it decides which toilets fit. Standard is 12 inches, but older homes vary. Water-saving models are available, and California rules limit flush volume on new toilets.

California flush limits

California efficiency rules limit new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush, so a replacement will use less water than a toilet from the 1960s or 1970s. Installation follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including the flange and the wax seal.

Quick checks before you call

Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking. Check that the water level sits about an inch below the overflow tube. Look for dampness at the supply line and the base.

If none of that explains it, a plumber will find the cause.
