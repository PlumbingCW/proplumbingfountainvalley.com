When a water line leak is an emergency

Water bubbling up in the yard, a sudden drop in pressure or a meter that spins with every fixture off all suggest a break in the buried line. Water at pressure can wash out soil, undermine pavers and run to a low spot near the house.

Close the main shutoff at the house. If the leak continues, the break is on the meter side of that valve, and the city's curb stop is the next point to close. Call (714) 750-8637 and the 24/7 line will guide you.

What the service line is

The service line starts at the water meter, which the city or water utility maintains, and runs underground to your home's main shutoff. In general the property owner is responsible for the section on the house side of the meter. Ask the City of Fountain Valley if you are unsure where the line of responsibility sits at your address.

Lines are commonly copper, galvanized steel or plastic, depending on when the house was built. Each fails in a different way and the repair depends on which one you have.

Signs of a failing water line

The buried line is out of sight, so these are the clues.

Wet or sunken patch

Water coming up in a yard or a depression in soil or pavers over the line.

Low or fluctuating pressure

A leak between the meter and the house lowers pressure at every fixture.

Rusty or discolored water

Corroded galvanized line sheds rust into the water.

Unexplained bill increase

A steady leak shows as a higher usage reading with no change in habits.

Finding the break

The plumber starts with a meter test and a pressure check on the line. A line tracer marks the route, and acoustic equipment listens for the leak. Digging begins only once the location is clear, which keeps the trench short.

If the leak is under a driveway or a patio, the plumber discusses trenchless replacement or rerouting around the hardscape. Leak detection is the first step if the break is not obvious.

Low water pressure and the service line

Pressure that drops over months, with no change in the street, can point to a narrowed or leaking service line. A galvanized line may be mostly rust inside. Pressure that is too high is also an issue, because it stresses the line and fixtures.

The plumber can measure pressure at a hose bib to see where you stand. A pressure regulator is common in California homes, and it may be due for replacement if the numbers look wrong.

Pressure limits and permits

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which the California Plumbing Code is built on, calls for a pressure regulator where pressure could exceed 80 psi. A new service line is usually permitted work with an inspection before the trench is closed, so ask the Building Division what applies.

Permits and the city

The City of Fountain Valley Building Division at 714-593-4429 can tell you whether your repair needs a permit. Work in the public right of way, such as at the curb, is handled differently from work on private property.

The plumber will mention what applies and how scheduling for inspection works.
