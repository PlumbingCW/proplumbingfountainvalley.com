When a water heater problem is an emergency

A leaking tank, a burst supply connection or water near the heater's electrical or gas controls is an emergency. No hot water is urgent in a different way, because it affects daily life but usually does not damage anything.

If water is leaking, switch off the heater at the breaker or set a gas heater's control to off, close the cold-water shutoff valve on top of the tank and keep people away from the wet floor. If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 first. Our 24/7 line is (714) 750-8637.

What fails on a water heater

Tank water heaters have a few parts that wear out. On gas units that includes the thermocouple, the gas control valve and the pilot assembly. On electric units it is the upper and lower heating elements and the thermostats. On both, the anode rod, the temperature and pressure relief valve and the drain valve age with time.

A plumber starts by identifying which of those parts matches the symptom. Water that is lukewarm and runs out fast often points to a failed lower element or a heavily scaled tank. Water that is hot for a minute and then cold often points to a broken dip tube.

Symptoms and what they usually mean

Match your symptom to the likely cause. A plumber confirms with tests before replacing anything.

No hot water at all

On electric units, a tripped breaker or failed upper element. On gas units, a pilot that will not stay lit or a bad thermocouple.

Water not hot enough

A failed lower element, a thermostat setting that drifted or sediment insulating the tank bottom.

Rumbling or popping

Mineral sediment cooking at the bottom of the tank. Common with hard water and often a sign the tank is aging.

Water around the base

A loose fitting or relief valve may be repairable. A leak from the tank body is not, and replacement is the answer.

Why scale matters in Fountain Valley

The city's 2026 water quality report lists average hardness of 217 milligrams per liter, which is 13 grains per gallon, with a range of 10 to 15. The city's own FAQ describes water with 10 grains or more as hard. Hard water leaves mineral scale on heating elements and the tank floor, and that scale is why elements burn out and tanks rumble.

Flushing a tank every year can slow the buildup, but sediment that has been left for years may not come out and flushing a very old tank can start a leak. Ask the plumber whether a flush is worthwhile for your unit. Persistent scale problems are also a reason to price a water softener.

Gas, electric and tankless units

Gas heaters need venting checks and combustion safety. If a gas heater has soot, scorch marks or a flame that is yellow instead of blue, the plumber will treat it as a safety issue before a comfort issue. Electric heaters need power testing with the circuit off, and elements are usually swapped as a pair.

Tankless units fail differently. Error codes, cold-water sandwiches and weak flow are often scale in the heat exchanger. Descaling is a common service, covered on our tankless water heater page.

No hot water: a quick check before you call

Check the breaker for an electric heater and the pilot light for a gas heater. Make sure the thermostat is not set to vacation mode. If the reset button on an electric unit has tripped, press it once, and if it trips again do not keep resetting it, because that points to a failed element or wiring fault.

If you reach the end of that list and still have no hot water, call. We note what you have already tried so the plumber can skip those steps.

Safety rules that apply to heaters

California law requires water heaters to be braced or strapped against earthquake movement, and the California Plumbing Code, based on the UPC, calls for a temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe. A repair visit is a good time to check both, since a loose strap or a capped relief line is easy to miss.

Check the relief valve line

A water heater has a temperature and pressure relief valve that opens when the tank gets too hot or too pressurized. A trickle or drip from the discharge pipe can mean the valve is failing, or it can mean thermal expansion that needs an expansion tank. Either way the valve should not be capped or plugged.

If the discharge pipe is running and the water is very hot, turn off the heater and call right away.
