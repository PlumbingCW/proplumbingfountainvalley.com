What we install

Common installs include kitchen and bathroom faucets, toilets, sinks, tubs and showers, garbage disposals, dishwasher and washing machine hookups, ice maker lines, hose bibs and water filtration lines. In a remodel, we also handle the rough-in, which is the pipes inside the walls that set where fixtures sit.

Remodels and new layouts

Moving a fixture means moving the drain, the vent and the supply, and that is when permits matter. The City of Fountain Valley Building Division, at 714-593-4429, can tell you which parts of your project need a permit and an inspection.

Bring your plan or a sketch to the walkthrough. A plumber can often suggest a position that saves on drain work.

Water quality and fixtures

The city's 2026 water quality report lists average hardness of 13 grains per gallon. Scale builds on faucet aerators, showerheads and inside cartridges. When you pick fixtures, choose ones with easy-clean aerators and replaceable cartridges.

If you are installing a filter or softener, plan the line and drain access first, and see our water softener and filtration page.

Choosing fixtures that last

Fixtures differ widely in how easy they are to repair. Look for replaceable cartridges, standard supply connections and parts that are sold separately. A beautiful faucet that cannot be repaired is a poor value when a cartridge fails.

Ask the plumber which brands they find easy to service. They see the failures that reviews do not.

What to have ready for the visit

Clear the area under sinks, move items away from the work zone and know where the main water shutoff is. If you bought fixtures, have the boxes and manuals on hand. Photos of the existing setup help the plumber plan.

If an electrician or a carpenter is also working, tell the dispatcher so the visits can be sequenced.

The codes behind new work

New fixtures and lines follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which sets pipe sizes, venting and fixture clearances. Moving or adding a fixture usually needs a permit and an inspection, while swapping a like-for-like faucet usually does not.

Outdoor and utility installs

Hose bibs, laundry connections and garage utility sinks are quick installs. Outdoor lines should have a shutoff and an accessible location. Where lines cross concrete, the plumber discusses options such as going overhead.

Backflow protection may be required on certain outdoor connections, and the plumber will tell you if it applies.
