Choosing a water heater type

The decision starts with what you have. A gas tank swapped for a gas tank is the simplest. Switching fuel type, adding a tankless unit or moving the heater changes the gas line, vent, electrical or water connections, and each adds work.

Household size and usage set the capacity. Space and venting set what is possible. The plumber measures the location, checks the existing connections and recommends options rather than a single product.

Hard water in the planning

Fountain Valley's 2026 water quality report lists average hardness of 217 milligrams per liter, or 13 grains per gallon. In the city's own words, hardness of 10 grains or more is considered hard. Scale builds on elements and tank floors, so ask about anode rod options, a yearly flush plan and a water softener.

Tankless units are more sensitive to scale, and descaling schedules matter more. Decide on the maintenance plan before you buy.

Permits, strapping and code

Water heater installations commonly need a plumbing permit, and California rules call for seismic strapping on tank heaters. The City of Fountain Valley Building Division at 10200 Slater Avenue handles permits, and you can reach it at 714-593-4429. Permit rules vary, so confirm what applies to your install.

The plumber can explain the work and the inspection, and a permitted install keeps the record clean for a future sale.

Code requirements for a new install

A new install follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which calls for a relief valve and discharge line, and California law requires bracing against earthquake movement. Gas units also need proper venting and combustion air, and electric units follow the California Electrical Code, based on the National Electrical Code (NEC).

Moving or relocating a heater

Relocating a heater means new gas, water and vent runs, and sometimes a new electrical circuit. It can be worth it to free a closet or to place the unit closer to the fixtures that use the most hot water.

Shorter pipe runs also mean less wait for hot water at the tap.
