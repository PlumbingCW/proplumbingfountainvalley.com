Santa Ana plumbing calls start with the age of the house

Santa Ana was incorporated on June 1, 1886 and is the county seat of Orange County, with about 310,000 residents at the 2020 census and roughly 27 square miles of land. A city that old and that large has every kind of building: historic districts such as French Park, mid-century neighborhoods, apartment blocks and newer infill near downtown.

That mix is why we ask three questions on every Santa Ana call. How old is the house, is it a rental or an owner-occupied home, and where did you first see the problem? The answers tell the dispatcher whether to send a crew ready for old galvanized supply lines, a long drain run or a tankless unit.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

Santa Ana residents often wonder what to do in the first few minutes. These four steps limit the damage while the 24/7 line arranges a plumber.

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff, often near the front hose bib or at the meter box, and turn it clockwise. Open one low faucet to drain the line.

Cut power near water

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel dry. Keep everyone away from outlets in standing water.

Clear the area

Lift rugs, move furniture and electronics, and keep children and pets out of the wet space.

Call and photograph

Ring (714) 750-8637 and photograph the damage for your landlord or insurance company.

Hard water in Santa Ana and what it does to heaters

According to the City of Santa Ana's water quality information, its groundwater has an average total hardness of 239 parts per million as calcium carbonate, about 14 grains per gallon, with a range from 130 to 307 parts per million. The city operates 21 groundwater wells, and the balance of its supply is imported water from the Metropolitan Water District.

Water at that hardness leaves scale on elements and tank floors, so Santa Ana water heaters often announce trouble with rumbling, popping or lukewarm water. Our water heater repair page lists the symptoms, and a tank near the end of its life is covered on the replacement page. Your hardness may differ from the average, so ask City of Santa Ana Water for the latest report.

Parts of Santa Ana worth knowing

The city has several distinct areas. The notes below describe places people use to say where they are.

Downtown and the civic center

The downtown core holds the Orange County administrative buildings and the Ronald Reagan Federal Building and Courthouse, along with older commercial buildings. Older buildings mean older drain and supply lines, and commercial plumbing calls are common.

French Park and the historic districts

The Downtown and French Park historic districts include homes of an age where original galvanized supply lines and clay or cast iron drains are possible. A plumber should look before assuming anything.

The east side near the zoo

The Santa Ana Zoo, built in 1960, sits on the east side of the city. Whatever the foundation type, a spinning meter or a damp floor is a reason for leak detection, and the plumber will check how your home is built.

South Coast Metro

The southeast end of the city shares the South Coast Metro area with Costa Mesa, and South Coast Plaza is its primary destination. Offices and retail here lean on after-hours service.

Drains and sewer lines in a city this size

The Santa Ana River runs through the west end of the city and is mostly channelized. Sewer laterals in Santa Ana connect to city mains, and an old lateral is a common reason a drain keeps clogging. When one fixture is slow, drain cleaning usually fixes it. When several back up, the plumber will likely recommend a camera look, which is explained on the sewer inspection page.

In a multi-unit building, a shared main can back up into the lowest unit first. If you rent, report the problem to your manager in writing the same day and call us if the situation is urgent.

Renters, landlords and property managers

Renters and landlords both call us, and a leak in one unit can reach another. Tenants should shut the water off at the unit if possible, document the damage and notify the landlord. Landlords and managers can call us directly to schedule access and to ask about repeated repairs.

We cannot say who is responsible for a given repair, since that depends on the lease and on state and local rules. We can tell you what is wrong, how it can be fixed and what it will cost before work starts.

Permits and the City of Santa Ana

Permit rules in Santa Ana are set by the city, not by Fountain Valley. If your project needs a permit, such as a water heater replacement or a repipe, contact the City of Santa Ana's building division to confirm what applies. The plumber can explain the work and the inspection.

Keep your invoice and any permit paperwork. They matter when you sell, and some insurers ask for them.
