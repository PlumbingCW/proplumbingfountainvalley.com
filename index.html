What Is Different About Plumbing In Fountain Valley

Fountain Valley was incorporated on June 13, 1957, and the city's own history page describes rapid growth through the 1960s. Homes from that period are now in their sixth decade, so water heaters, shutoff valves, supply lines and drain lines have usually been replaced at least once, and sometimes by different owners using different materials.

When a plumber opens a wall in a house like that, mixed pipe types are normal, and a good diagnosis starts with finding out what is really there.

The water itself also matters. The city reports six wells and one imported water connection, and in 2025 it supplied groundwater only. The Orange County Water District's Groundwater Replenishment System, on its campus at 18700 Ward Street in Fountain Valley, supplies a share of the basin's annual refill. Whatever the source, the hardness numbers explain why scale builds up inside water heaters and around faucet aerators, and why a water softener or filter is a common question.

The same page of the city's report lists average lead at the tap of 6 parts per billion at the 90th percentile, with none of 33 sampled homes above the action level of 15. If you want to know more about what is in your own tap, the city's water quality report and the city's water division are the place to ask.

Before We Arrive

What To Do While You Wait For An Emergency Plumber

The first ten minutes after a pipe bursts or a drain backs up decide how much damage the house takes. These four steps work for most emergency calls in Fountain Valley homes.

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff valve, usually near the street side of the house or at the garage wall.

Protect the power

If water is near outlets or the panel, leave that area and switch off power only if you can do it dry.

Stop using drains

For a backup, do not flush or run sinks, washers or dishwashers until the line is cleared.

Take photos

Document the damage for your insurance company before you move anything.
