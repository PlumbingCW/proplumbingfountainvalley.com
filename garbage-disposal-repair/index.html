When a disposal problem is an emergency

A disposal leaking from the body or the drain flange puts water into the cabinet, and a disposal that backs up the sink can overflow onto the counter. Turn off the disposal at its wall switch and unplug it or switch off the breaker before reaching in.

Never put a hand in the disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object. Call (714) 750-8637 if water is escaping. The line is open 24/7, and a plumber will repair or replace the unit.

Why disposals fail

A hum without motion means the flywheel is jammed, and most jams clear with a hex key turned from the bottom of the unit. A dead unit with no hum is usually the reset button or a tripped breaker. Leaks come from the sink flange seal, the dishwasher inlet, the drain outlet or a crack in the housing.

Grease and fibrous food, such as celery and potato peel, are common causes of both jams and slow drains. A disposal is not a trash can, and the line below it still has to carry what it grinds.

Common symptoms and causes

Match the symptom to the likely cause.

Hums, does not spin

Jammed flywheel. Safe to free with the unit off and unplugged.

Completely dead

Reset button tripped, breaker off or a failed motor.

Water under the unit

Failed seal, loose connection or cracked housing. A cracked housing means replacement.

Foul odor

Buildup on the grinding chamber or a slow drain below.

Backups that look like disposal problems

If water rises in the sink when the disposal runs, or if it comes up in the other basin, the clog may be in the drain line below. The disposal does not fix a blocked line, and running it can make a mess. See our drain cleaning page for how the kitchen line is cleared.

A dishwasher that drains into the disposal can also back up into the dishwasher. If the dishwasher fills with dirty water, tell the plumber when you call.

Installing a new disposal

A replacement is usually a same-visit job: the old unit is disconnected, the mounting ring is reset, and the new unit is wired or plugged in, connected to the drain and tested. If you want a disposal where none exists, an electrical outlet and a wall switch are needed, and the plumber will tell you if that requires an electrician.

Horsepower, sound insulation and continuous or batch feed are the main choices. A larger motor helps with tough waste but does not replace good habits.

Electrical and drain rules

Disposal wiring follows the California Electrical Code, which is based on the National Electrical Code (NEC), and the drain connection follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). A dishwasher drain that enters the disposal should loop high or use an air gap, a detail the plumber checks.

What not to grind

Grease, coffee grounds in quantity, pasta and rice that swell, bones, fibrous vegetables and fruit pits all cause trouble. Run cold water while the unit operates and for 15 seconds after.

Ice cubes and citrus peel freshen a unit but do not fix a deep clog.
